# Sceptic's verdicts (12 October 2026)

Verdicts on the Chapter 4 findings. I checked each quote against chapter_04-as-built.md, chapter_04-after-the-edit.md and chapter_04.py. I checked the factual claims against Chapters 1 to 3, the engine (`shop/records.py` `owners`, `shop/week.py`) and docs/plan.md.

1. PARTLY. The substance holds. The closing says "The requirement is met under some readings and not others". The requirement is "Each asset", and 2 of the 7 assets fail all three columns, so no reading is met for every asset. The page's own "Of the 7 assets, 5 have one owner" is accurate, and chapter-04.md's "as written, for 5 of 7 assets" is the correct framing. The claimed self-contradiction is an over-call. "Any list meets some readings" and "no owner under any reading" refer to different things (a list, and two files), so they do not contradict each other.

2. UPHELD. Chapter 1 says the export "writes three files to object storage", and `shop/week.py` writes all three, `products.csv` included ("The export: three files, two minutes apart"). Chapter 2's listing shows `products.csv` modified two minutes after `customers.jsonl`, and Chapter 3 says "the export copies it [updated_by] into the file". So "Those two are the files the export writes" wrongly implies `products.csv` is not an export file, and it gives a cause the table does not show. The same phrase appears in "Before Chapter 5" and in the chapter notes.

3. UPHELD. The `yours` cell is fixed text. It drops Chapter 3's `day means` and `revenue_pence means` lines. It adds four lines the reader never wrote: `clean_customers responsible`, `clean_orders responsible`, `clean_orders drops` and `products.csv responsible`. "You have your records from Chapter 3 ... your records first" is therefore untrue of the cell. A reader who edited their Chapter 3 record sees none of it carried over.

4. PARTLY. The quotes are accurate, and no source is given for the `drops` line. The "rule that explains Thursday" is only half right, because only the no-customer-id condition explains it. The claim that Chapter 4 states as fact what Chapter 2 says cannot be known is overstated. Chapter 2 itself asserts the design, "four rules", including "drop orders with a quantity of 0 or less". What it says cannot be told is whether the rule exists, and that is "from the data". A human-written `drops` line is a record made at the time, which fits Chapter 2's argument. What holds is the missing source (who says it?) and the two conditions merged into one line when only one has been tested.

5. PARTLY. The staff-list facts check out. The reader is the data engineer (Chapter 2: "your first morning as the shop's data engineer"; Chapter 3: the cleaned tables "are now yours"). There is no staff line for them, `k.adeyemi` or `r.novak`. A reader who made the data engineer a person would get `one_person` 5 for the five assets with one person, not 3, because the `clean_*` rows would flip to yes. "A later section of the chapter returns to this" is only weakly kept. The `owners` lead-in does say the cell makes the data engineer a team, so the choice is not hidden. But it is not framed as a choice, and the alternative and its effect are never given. The "hidden choice" wording is too strong, and the lead-in does state the choice.

6. UPHELD. The page never says that "owner" now means the value of `responsible`. Chapter 2 set the warehouse's account (`etl_service`) as the "owner" that meets "Every table must have an owner" "as written", and Chapter 3 defined `responsible` as "who to ask about it". The account reading is neither offered nor dismissed in Chapter 4. Only the notes say why it is set aside. The word shifts between the objectives, the requirement, the cells and the `owners` output column.

7. UPHELD. The prediction asks for an exact number of people, which breaks the rule that a count is offered as explanations. The cell that answers it is `COUNT(DISTINCT value)` and gives 4 values, so no cell computes a number of people. A reader's 3 or 4 is never compared with anything. The list one cell above already shows both spellings. "The next cell asks" gives the cell a voice. The sub-point about "before you run the cell after it" against "Run all" is the course-wide prediction pattern, and I count it as weak.

8. UPHELD. Chapter 3 defines "A record is a set of lines". Chapter 4 uses "record" for one line in "that every record uses for it", in the check message "no record describes such a thing", and in "whether a record is true" and "Which record on that platform...". It also uses "your records" for the whole, and the check output's column is named `record` but holds a line. The definition of identifier turns on this. Chapter 3's check message ("The records do not describe...") is itself inconsistent, which worsens the problem but does not excuse it.

9. UPHELD. The objectives say "Rewrite the records", "Write a model" and "merge two spellings". The typed records and the model are supplied complete, and the reader edits two values and runs cells.

10. PARTLY. "You have two choices" has no cell, and no experiment shows the check's limit, so that part is accurate. "Anyone who keeps the records to the model loses it" is awkward English. CLAUDE.md permits a stated conclusion, so a missing experiment is an optional improvement, not a fault.

11. UPHELD (minor). (a) In the after-the-edit page, the output shows 3 problems under the prose "The check finds 5 problems. Of these, 4...", and a reload from the saved copy would do the same. (b) The strings `responsible: Jo Marsh` and `responsible: Sam Lund` also appear in the `yours` cell and in the `after` cell's `.replace(...)` arguments, so a wrong-cell edit is possible. (c) The cell applies the edits itself, so "reads the records after your two changes" does not hold for a reader who made none. (d) Lines 3 and 11 are identical after the edit. This is the weakest of the four, because the page does say that both lines "now give `j.marsh`".

12. UPHELD. The page never maps the bullets to `one_owner`, `one_person` and `one_team`. In the engine, `one_owner` is exactly one value and `one_team` is exactly one team plus exactly one value, and the output shows them identical on all seven rows. The third reading is about how values are counted, not what "owner" means. The three readings are listed before the reader commits, which departs from the CLAUDE.md order of commit first, then show the readings. "Ask what the list is for" is not offered. Predicting which assets fail is a suggestion, not a defect.

13. PARTLY. These points hold. The dashboard shares the dataset's attributes in the model (`means`, `responsible`) yet is excluded on the ground of holding no rows. Entity omits the dashboard. "Their columns" is listed as a dataset attribute, but the model has none. The tie between a column and its table is only a dot in the identifier, and no check tests it. This point is not an error: "relationship type" is introduced in "A model of the records", so "used without being introduced" is wrong for it. "References" is loosely used. The need for a type on every line is only asserted. The missing link to Chapter 3's schema is a nicety, not a fault.

14. PARTLY. No cell counts people after the merge, so "the records can count people instead of spellings" is a capability that is never shown. That part holds. "The checks" in the plural is a trivial nit.

15. PARTLY. "and never sometimes Jo Marsh" is garbled. The over-assertion is weaker. Table names are unique within the shop, and the closing note already flags that logins can be reassigned. The `analytics` schema point is real but small.

16. PARTLY. These points hold. "Run the next cell to list every line that has `responsible` in it" is loose, since the SQL filters `field = 'responsible'`. The paragraph repeats the same point twice ("each is a piece of text", "each name is still a piece of text"). "As written, `daily_sales` has two owners" appears before owner means `responsible`. The Chapter 6 claim for `drops` is unsupported: the plan's Chapter 6 is "made from" edges through jobs, not cleaning rules. "Chapter 4 writes each line..." and "A model decides..." are marginal stylistic over-calls.

17. REJECTED as a separate finding. The facts are true (two lines in the staff list, and `k.adeyemi` and `r.novak` are absent), but the reviewer concedes it is "not an error". The reader-absent part is already covered by 5. The `team finance name: Finance` point is trivial and speculative.

Findings 1, 4, 5, 10, 13, 14, 15 and 16 are partly right and each needs trimming. Finding 1 holds on the "met under some readings" wording but not on the claimed contradiction. Finding 4's contradiction with Chapter 2 is overstated. Finding 5's "hidden choice" is overstated. The "relationship type not introduced" part of 13 is wrong. Finding 17 is rejected.
