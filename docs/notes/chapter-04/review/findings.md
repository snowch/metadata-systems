## Blocking

1. **The closing claims a reading is met that no reading is.** "Before Chapter 5": "The requirement is met under some readings and not others, and the two files the export writes have no owner under any reading." Section '"Each asset has one owner."': "Any list meets some readings and not others, so the list should say which ones it meets." The requirement is "Each asset", and the table shows 2 of 7 assets failing under all three columns (no | no | no). So the requirement as a whole is met under no reading; some readings are met for 5 or 3 of the 7 assets. The two sentences also contradict each other ("some readings", then "any reading"). Direction: say per reading how many of the 7 assets meet it, and that none is met for every asset. The notes' own wording ("as written, for 5 of 7 assets") is the accurate one.

2. **The two files without an owner are described as "the files the export writes".** "Of the 7 assets ... Those two are the files the export writes, and the records do not say who is responsible for them." Repeated in "Before Chapter 5". Chapter 1 says the export "writes three files to object storage", and Chapter 3 says the export copies `updated_by` into `products.csv`. `products.csv` is an export file too, and it has an owner here. The phrase gives a cause the table does not support and Chapter 1 contradicts. Direction: name the two files and say only what the records show (no `responsible` line), without attributing it to the export.

3. **"You have your records from Chapter 3" is not true of the cell that follows.** Section "Who owns what": "You have your records from Chapter 3 ... The first cell puts both together, in Chapter 3's notation: your records first, then Jo's notes." The `yours` cell is fixed text, not what the reader wrote. It drops two of Chapter 3's five lines (`daily_sales day means`, `daily_sales revenue_pence means`) and adds four lines the reader never wrote (`clean_customers responsible`, `clean_orders responsible`, `clean_orders drops`, `products.csv responsible`). A reader who changed their Chapter 3 record sees none of it carried over. Direction: say these are the records as Chapter 3 left them plus what the head said, make each line's source explicit, or do not call them the reader's own.

4. **The `drops` line states as fact what Chapter 2 says cannot be known.** Section "A fact the model has no type for": "The line is the rule that explains Thursday's low revenue in Chapter 2." The line also says `clean_orders` drops "orders with ... a quantity of 0 or less". Chapter 2 ("A rule the week never tests"): the rule "never removes a row" and "From the data, you cannot tell whether the rule exists"; the shop's design is something "the platform does not record and you cannot see". Nothing in Chapters 2 or 3 gives the reader a source for this line; Chapter 3's head only says the cleaned tables "are now yours". The no-customer-id half does explain Thursday; the other half is untested. The section then says "No check can say whether that text is true", which applies to the reader's own line. Direction: give the line a source (who says it), or split the two conditions and say which the week confirms; keep fact and claim apart.

5. **"The data engineer" is left unresolved, a promise is not kept, and a hidden choice decides the `one_person` result.** Section "A model of the records": "Whether it should name a person or a team depends on what \"one owner\" means, and a later section of the chapter returns to this." No later section does. The `owners` cell then silently "makes the data engineer a team with the identifier `data_engineering`", and the `one_person` column (3, both cleaned tables `no`) follows from that choice alone; the prose says only "The two cleaned tables name a team", as if the records said so. The reader is the data engineer (Chapter 2: "your first morning as the shop's data engineer"; Chapter 3: the cleaned tables "are now yours"), yet the staff list "has two lines" with none for them, nor for `k.adeyemi` and `r.novak`, whom Chapter 3's `updated_by` count showed. A reader who made themselves a person would get `one_person` 5. Direction: state the choice as the shop's, with the alternative and its effect, or let the reader make it; place the reader and the other logins in the staff list or say why they are absent; delete the signpost to "a later section".

6. **"Owner" changes meaning without notice.** Objectives: "Count the owners that the records name". Section "Who owns what": the requirement says "owner"; the cells use `responsible`; the output column is `AS owners`. Chapter 2 showed the warehouse's `table_owner` (`etl_service`, an account) and said "Every table must have an owner" was "met as written" by it; Chapter 3 defined `responsible` as "who to ask about it". Chapter 4 never says that owner now means the value of `responsible`, or why the warehouse's account is set aside (the notes say so; the page does not). A reader taking "Each asset has one owner" literally can say the three tables already pass. All three readings are about `responsible`; the account reading from Chapter 2 is neither offered nor dismissed. Direction: say once that the list is of who is responsible, that the warehouse's account answers a different question, and use one word for the field's content throughout.

7. **The first prediction asks about people, but the cell that answers it counts values.** "The next cell asks how many people are responsible for the shop's assets, according to these records." The cell is `COUNT(DISTINCT value)`, giving 4. Nothing computes a count of people, so a reader's number cannot be right or wrong against it; the page then says "The count is of spellings, not people". A reader who answers 3 (me, Jo, Sam; the reader is the data engineer) is never shown whether 3 is the number of people, and a reader who answers 4 is told the same thing. Also: a number is requested (CLAUDE.md: a count is offered as explanations, never an exact figure; no "I can't tell" option); the list one cell above already shows both spellings; "its blanks" and "the next cell asks" give the cell a voice; and "before you run the cell after it" conflicts with the box's "Run every cell first": after Run all, the `4` and the paragraph beginning "The cell counts 4 different values" sit directly under the blanks. Direction: predict something the cell can show (how many distinct values, or whether `daily_sales` has one value), then ask about people against independent evidence (the head's statement, the staff list).

8. **"Record" is used for a line, and also for the whole set, after Chapter 3 defined it as the set.** Chapter 3: "A record is a set of lines of text that you write and keep beside the data." Chapter 4, "What the records describe": an identifier is a value "that every record uses for it"; the check says "no record describes such a thing"; closing: "whether a record is true", "Which record on that platform could a check accept while the record is false?". There `record` is a single line. The page also says "the records" for the cell's contents and "your records" for the whole, and the check's rows are called `record`. The definition of identifier, the chapter's key term, turns on which. Direction: use "line" for one line, and keep "records" (or Chapter 3's "record") for the whole; recheck the check message wording.
