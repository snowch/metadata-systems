# Brief BX: decide first, then open the readings

Read `docs/style.md` for the voice: British English, plain, short, no em dashes, no filler. Write
each string under its own `## BXn` heading, exactly as it will appear, and nothing else.

## Context

Chapter 2: the head of the shop asked whom to ask when a dashboard number looks wrong; you turned
that into the rule "Every table must have an owner."; the warehouse's `table_owner` holds
`etl_service` for every table, so the rule is met as written. Next comes a list of four readings of
"owner". Until now a code cell held two questions for the reader to type answers into ("What would
you send the head?" and "What do you take "owner" to mean?"); nothing used the answers. The cell
goes. The list of readings now sits inside a closed block that the reader opens by tapping its
title.

## BX1: one sentence in a box above the closed block

Ask the reader to decide two things before they open the readings: what they would send the head,
and what they take "owner" to mean. Say the readings are in the block below, which they open.

## BX2: the closed block's title, a few words

Names what the block holds: four readings of "owner". No full stop.
