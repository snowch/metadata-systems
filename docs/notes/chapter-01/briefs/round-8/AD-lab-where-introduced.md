# Brief AD: explain the lab where the chapter first names it

Read `docs/notes/chapter-01/briefs/round-4/common.md` first, and the files it names: every rule
there holds for this brief, and `docs/style.md`'s rule "silence is preferable to filler" above all.
Write your draft to `docs/notes/chapter-01/drafts/round-8/AD.md`.

The author found two faults. The chapter names the lab in its opening section without explaining
it ("Every figure on this page runs the Metadata Lab: a small data platform that holds the shop
and runs in your browser. The lab works out everything a figure shows."), and then a long note on
the lab repeated behind every figure's badge and again at the foot of the page. The note is gone
from both. The lab is now explained once, in the opening section, where the reader first meets it:
after the paragraphs on the shop's systems, its assets and its nights, and before the map.

## qLab

The lab's paragraphs, in Markdown, names in backticks. Two short paragraphs, no list, no heading,
no more than 170 words. The facts are in `docs/notes/chapter-01/facts.md`, under "The Metadata
Lab, where the chapter introduces it", numbered 1 to 6. Carry every one of them, in that order:

- first paragraph: facts 1, 2 and 3, what the lab is, what it holds, and the week;
- second paragraph: facts 4, 5 and 6, how it runs, how you use it, and what your browser keeps.

The paragraphs above these already say what that section of the facts lists: do not say any of it
again. In particular, do not name the three systems, the seven assets or what an asset is, and do
not say again that the files are written each night or that you cannot see the programs.

Rules:

- Name each thing before a sentence uses it with "the". No sentence may say "the week" before the
  sentence that gives the week's dates.
- Put the subject first in every sentence.

Do not:

- say how many programs there are, which asset a program reads or writes, or what any program
  does;
- use these words: metadata (except in the name "the Metadata Lab"), simulate or simulation,
  schema, dataset, job, event, lineage, environment; or "run" as a noun (as a verb it is fine).
