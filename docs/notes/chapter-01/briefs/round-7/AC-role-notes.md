# Brief AC: what an experiment's and an instrument's badges say

Read `docs/notes/chapter-01/briefs/round-4/common.md` first, and the files it names: every rule
there holds for this brief. Read `docs/style.md`'s rule "silence is preferable to filler" twice:
this brief exists because of it. Write your draft to `docs/notes/chapter-01/drafts/round-7/AC.md`.

Each figure's badge opens a note: first a line on what that kind of figure asks you to do, then
the note on the lab. The author found the line for a reference figure was filler ("You have what
you were told about the platform, kept to hand. It asks nothing of you.") and it is gone: a
reference figure's badge now opens the lab's note alone. The lines for the other two kinds are
these, and parts of them are filler too ("the lab's evidence answers", "What you take away is
what the evidence shows beside what you expected", "answers no question by itself"):

> experiment: You commit first, to a prediction, a choice or a query you build. Then you act, and
> the lab's evidence answers. What you take away is what the evidence shows beside what you
> expected.
>
> inspect: This is an instrument: it shows what the platform holds, and answers no question by
> itself. Use it on the question the page has just asked, and look for the evidence that question
> needs.

Write each as instructions for the learner's next action, in Markdown, one or two short sentences,
with no sentence the learner would not miss. Write them as a list, `experiment:` then `inspect:`.

## roleNotes

Facts, in this order:

- experiment: commit first, to a prediction, a choice or a query you build; then compare what the
  lab shows with what you expected.
- inspect: it shows what the platform holds; use it to answer the question just above it, and
  look in it for the evidence that question needs.

Do not describe the lab or a figure as a person (it does not "ask", "answer" or "wait"), do not
reassure, and do not say what the learner has been told or already knows.
