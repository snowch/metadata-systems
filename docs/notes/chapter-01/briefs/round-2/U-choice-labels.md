# Brief U: the two labels of a choice

Read `common.md` in this directory first. Write your draft to
`docs/notes/chapter-01/drafts/round-2/U.md`.

A figure now asks some questions as a choice, not a prediction: the learner says what they would
do in the shop's place, presses a button, and the lab shows what the shop does beside their
choice. Nothing calls the choice right or wrong. A prediction's button says "Check my prediction"
and its options are grouped under the name "Prediction"; a choice needs its own two labels. Both
are plain text, a few words, no full stop.

## checkChoice

The button the learner presses once they have chosen. Facts: pressing it keeps their choice and
shows what the shop does. It must not say "check", "correct" or "prediction": nothing is checked.

## yourChoice

The name of the group of options, read out by a screen reader, not shown. Facts: it is the
learner's choice. Two or three words.
