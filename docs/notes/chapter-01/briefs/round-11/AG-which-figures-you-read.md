## labUse

Markdown: one paragraph, no list, no heading, no more than 85 words. In this order:

- the first three figures (the map, the shop's week and its dashboard) only show you the shop:
  you read them;
- in each of the others you ask the lab something: say how, with the actions on the page (you
  choose an answer, an asset or the parts of a query, and press a button);
- the lab works out the result from the shop's data and shows it in the figure;
- many of them ask you to predict or choose before the lab answers;
- you cannot see the shop's programs, only what they leave behind, so you work out what happened
  from what the figures show you.

Rules:

- Do not name any button's words: the figures show them.
- Do not use these words: SQL, engine, memory, page load, code, data model, underlying,
  interactive, metadata (except in the name "the Metadata Lab"), simulate, schema, dataset, job,
  event, lineage, instrument; or "run" as a noun (as a verb it is fine).
- Do not say how many programs there are, which asset a program reads or writes, or what any
  program does.
- Put the subject first in every sentence. No reassurance, no motivation, and nothing that gives
  the lab or a figure a wish, a mood or a wait, as a person would have.
